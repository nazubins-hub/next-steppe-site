A sample project, designed for a friend's property to put the full process through a real site.

**The site.** Two beds, about 850 ft² together, around the paver driveway of a north-facing lot in Vail. The lidar surface puts the larger bed at about 3.5 hours of direct sun and the smaller one at almost none: this is a shade garden at 9,480 ft effective elevation. Both beds sit 16–28 ft from the house, inside the zone where CSU Extension FS 6.305 admits only plants rated hard to ignite.

**The scope.** A one-inch soil exchange across both beds. Existing perennials are lifted, held and replanted in sections the same day, so roughly 70% of the bed is salvaged and 30% is new stock. Four shrubs in the small bed are swapped out.

**The planting.** 21 species chosen per bed for shade, effective elevation and fire rating, among them goldenbanner, silvery lupine, Colorado blue columbine, prairie smoke, pussytoes and native onions. Every plant is placed on the plan: structure first, groundcover last.
